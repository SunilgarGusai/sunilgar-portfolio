What is Vertex Eccentricity Labeled Energy (VELE)?

A mathematical introduction to Vertex Eccentricity Labeled Energy (VELE), its matrix definition, a complete-graph example, and the original 2025 publication.

Mathematical definition

Let G = (V, E) be a finite, simple, connected, undirected graph with n vertices. The eccentricity e(v) of a vertex v is the greatest graph distance from v to any other vertex of G.

e(v) = max { dG(v,u) : u ∈ V(G) }.

For vertices v1, …, vn, the Vertex Eccentricity Labeled (VEL) matrix is a real symmetric matrix with zero diagonal and off-diagonal entries equal to the sum of the corresponding eccentricities:

mij = { 0, if i = j; e(vi) + e(vj), if i ≠ j }.

If its eigenvalues are ξ1, …, ξn, the Vertex Eccentricity Labeled Energy is defined by

VELE(G) = |ξ1| + ··· + |ξn|.

Because the VEL matrix is symmetric, its eigenvalues are real. Its trace is zero because the diagonal entries vanish.

A checkable example: the complete graph

For the complete graph Kn, with n ≥ 2, every vertex has eccentricity 1. Consequently, its VEL matrix is 2(J − I), where J is the all-ones matrix and I is the identity matrix.

The eigenvalues are 2(n − 1), once, and −2, with multiplicity n − 1. Therefore:

VELE(Kn) = 4(n − 1).

For instance, VELE(K4) = 12. This example follows directly from the matrix definition; it does not require a computational approximation.

What does VELE capture?

VELE is a spectral invariant built from vertex eccentricities, which depend on global shortest-path distances. It is therefore different from conventional adjacency-matrix energy and should not be treated as interchangeable with it.

The original paper develops algebraic properties, bounds, graph-family formulas, and results under graph operations. The invariant describes a mathematical feature of graph structure; it does not by itself establish physical reliability, electrical security, or predictive performance in an applied network.

For an application that explicitly tests those limitations, see the VELE power-grid screening research explainer.
