Can Vertex Eccentricity Labeled Energy help screen power-grid vulnerability?

A source-linked overview of VELE power-grid vulnerability screening across six IEEE/MATPOWER cases, including DC-flow comparisons, negative findings, and reproducibility.

Research explainer · Sunilgar L. Gusai · 30 October 2026 · Academic portfolio

The research question

Power-grid topology and electrical severity are different things. A graph-based structural diagnostic can identify a topologically important element without showing that its failure necessarily causes severe physical consequences.

The VELE study examines whether an eccentricity-sensitive spectral descriptor adds useful information for screening contingencies, and where it agrees or disagrees with DC power-flow redistribution, residual disconnection, and service consequences. For the mathematical definition, see What is VELE?

How is the study organized?

Each benchmark is represented twice: as a simple graph for spectral/topological screening, and as a physical network retaining electrical attributes such as reactance, branch rows, phase-related parameters and generation/demand information. The study does not identify the two representations as interchangeable.

  • Six IEEE/MATPOWER benchmark systems: 14, 30, 39, 57, 118 and 300 buses.
  • 558 stylized single-bus removal cases and 784 physical branch-outage cases.
  • 1,692 progressive electrical states.
  • Independent intact-system DC-equation checks against MATPOWER 8.1 in GNU Octave.

Bus removals are stylized screening events rather than ordinary N−1 compliance assessments. Candidate-line screening is also separated from claims about downstream engineering feasibility.

Important limitations

  • Parallel circuits: in a simple graph, losing one circuit while a parallel circuit survives can leave the structural graph unchanged. The record reports 22 branch rows in parallel pairs with zero VELE stress despite nonzero DC-flow change.
  • Physical model dependence: DC-flow and redispatch assumptions affect how electrical severity is evaluated. They are not full AC security studies.
  • Structural versus electrical endpoints: residual disconnection is a topological outcome; it is not independent evidence of real-world electrical performance.
  • Generalizability: these are six benchmark systems and specified scenarios, not a claim of universal ranking quality.

The appropriate interpretation is that VELE can be assessed as a complementary structural screening diagnostic, not a substitute for electrical contingency analysis.
